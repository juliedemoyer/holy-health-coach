## The 10

### 1. **The Real Science of Sport Podcast** — Ross Tucker, Mike Finch

**What it is:** South African exercise physiologist Ross Tucker (PhD,
former Discovery sports scientist) breaks down endurance science with
journalist Mike Finch. *The* serious science podcast for endurance.
No-BS, anti-hype, frequently calls out wellness-industry nonsense.

**Concrete takeaways:**
- **Pacing wins marathons, not fitness.** Tucker's data on major marathons /
  Boston / NYC consistently shows that women who PB negative-split or
  even-split. The fastest marathoners run their second half 5–15 sec/km
  *faster* than their first. Practice this on your tune-up half.
- **Heat tolerance is trainable in 10–14 days.** An autumn city marathon can hit
  18–22°C. Three sauna sessions per week from T-21 add measurable heat
  acclimation. Cheap, evidence-based, ignored by 95% of amateurs.
- **The 80/20 rule is real and underused by amateurs.** ~80% of weekly
  km should be at conversational pace. Most amateur marathoners run
  60-70% easy and wonder why their tempo runs flatten. Use Coach's HR
  bands to enforce easy = easy.

**Best episode:** "Marathon Pacing — what 50 years of WR data
shows" (around their season 4 archives).

### 2. **Trained** — Nike Sports Science (Jaclyn Byrer)

**What it is:** Nike's flagship sports-science show. Interviews Olympians,
sports physiologists, mental-performance coaches. Production-quality
high. Hosted by Jaclyn Byrer who runs sub-3 herself.

**Concrete takeaways:**
- **Visualisation is rehearsal, not motivation.** Several episodes
  (Eliud Kipchoge, Shalane Flanagan) make the same point: visualise
  the *specific* hard parts (km 32 cramping, km 38 doubt), not the
  finish line. The brain needs the rehearsal before race day.
- **Female-athlete sleep needs an extra 30 min in late luteal phase.**
  Stacy Sims episode — luteal-phase recovery is harder; budget for it.
  Mind has noted this in his protocols.
- **Race-week stillness > race-week tweaks.** Whatever's been working
  in training, *don't change it.* Don't try a new gel, new shoe, new
  warm-up. Race-week is the Don't Touch It week.

**Best episode:** Anything with Shalane Flanagan or the Stacy Sims
"Women Are Not Small Men" episode.

### 3. **Magness & Marcus on Coaching** — Steve Magness, Jonathan Marcus

**What it is:** Two coaches deconstruct elite endurance training in
real-time. Magness coaches HOKA NAZ Elite, wrote *The Science of
Running* and *Do Hard Things*. The closest thing to sitting in the
coaches' room at Mammoth.

**Concrete takeaways:**
- **"Toughness isn't suppression — it's clarity."** Magness's central
  thesis: real athletic toughness is feeling the discomfort fully and
  *choosing the response*, not gritting through it. This is exactly
  what Mind teaches; the podcast makes it practicable.
- **Specificity beats volume in the last 8 weeks.** In the final block, your
  long runs should *look like race day*: same time of day, same
  conditions, same fuel cadence, same shoes. Not just "long."
- **Adapt the plan to the body, not the body to the plan.** When HRV
  crashes, change the plan. Most amateurs do the opposite. Coach's
  ACWR tile exists for this.

**Best episode:** "Marathon Specificity — why your last 8 weeks matter
most" or anything titled with "tapering."

### 4. **Inside Running Podcast** — Brad Beer, Brady Threlfall, Julian Spence

**What it is:** Three Australian sub-2:30 marathoners + a sports
physio. Conversational, technical, lots of laughs. Australian elite
marathon scene is one of the world's best right now (Brett Robinson,
Jess Stenson).

**Concrete takeaways:**
- **The two-week-out long run is the whole build's diagnostic.** If
  you can run 32 km strong with the last 15 km at MP, you can run
  race day. If you can't, no amount of taper will save it.
- **Caffeine timing matters more than dose.** 3 mg/kg, 45–60 min before
  start. Not "more is better" — there's a sweet spot. Practice it on
  your race-rehearsal long run, not on race day.
- **Easy days are sacred, not optional.** Brad's repeated point: the
  hard sessions can only be hard if the easy days are easy. Most
  amateur marathon failures are easy-day failures masquerading as
  workout failures.

**Best episode:** Their "Marathon Pacing — get it right" series.

### 5. **Some Work, All Play** — David & Megan Roche

**What it is:** Husband-and-wife couple coaching elite trail and road
runners. David Roche is a researcher + coach; Megan is an MD who runs
sub-2:50 marathons. Warm, joyful, science-grounded. The opposite of
toxic-grit.

**Concrete takeaways:**
- **Recovery isn't earned — it's required.** The Roches frame deload
  weeks as the *productive* weeks. Adaptation happens in recovery,
  not training. Coach already calls cutbacks "deload" — listen for
  reinforcement.
- **Fuel the fun.** Megan's repeated point: female athletes who
  under-fuel become brittle female athletes. Build phases are not
  weight-loss phases. Period.
- **Sleep is the cheapest performance enhancer.** 30 extra minutes
  per night beats any gel, any shoe, any intervention. Mah's classic
  study (basketball, stretched to endurance): two extra weeks of +1h
  sleep before competition = measurable performance gain.

**Best episode:** Anything tagged "marathon training" or
"female athletes." Their Eliud-Kipchoge analyses are gold.

### 6. **The Strength Running Podcast** — Jason Fitzgerald

**What it is:** Strength + injury prevention for distance runners.
Jason Fitzgerald is a coach + 2:39 marathoner. Practical, evidence-based,
treats injury prevention as the central problem of running.

**Concrete takeaways:**
- **2× strength sessions per week, non-negotiable.** Already in your
  goals.md. Fitzgerald's data: runners who keep strength through the
  build PB more often and injure less. Compound lifts: squat,
  deadlift, single-leg work.
- **Calves and Achilles are the failure points in marathon builds.**
  By Wks 12–14, watch for Achilles tightness. Eccentric calf raises
  3× per week, year-round. Bullet-proofs the weakest link.
- **Mobility ≠ stretching.** Hip mobility (90/90, world's greatest
  stretch) preserves stride length under fatigue. Static hamstring
  stretches do nothing measurable.

**Best episode:** "Strength training for marathoners" (multi-part).

### 7. **The Marathon Talk Podcast** — Tom Williams, Martin Yelling

**What it is:** UK marathon institution. Tom Williams (Parkrun) and
Martin Yelling (coach, ex-international). Long-running show, deep
archive. British, dry, warm.

**Concrete takeaways:**
- **Carb-load with attention to gut tolerance.** Two days at 8–10 g/kg
  (scale to your own body mass). Practice on the rehearsal long-run
  weekend. Some athletes' guts can't tolerate it; better to know now
  than at km 18 of race day.
- **The half-marathon tune-up race tells you the marathon time.** McMillan
  conversion: half-marathon time × 2.11 ≈ marathon time. If your
  August tune-up half is 1:36, race day = 3:23 estimate. This is a
  more reliable predictor than any Riegel calculation.
- **Don't taper if you're already injured.** Three weeks of partial
  rest with niggles is worse than two weeks of cancelling and resetting
  for spring. Tough call but better than DNF.

**Best episode:** Their annual race day race-week episodes (typically
mid-September each year). Listen to last year's.

### 8. **The Knowledge** — Coach Hadley

**What it is:** Coach Hadley (UK) breaks down the science of running
in 15-minute episodes. Concise, well-cited, practical. *The* show for
"I have 15 minutes between meetings, teach me something."

**Concrete takeaways:**
- **Threshold pace ≠ tempo pace.** "Tempo" is comfortably hard,
  threshold is *uncomfortably* hard. Both are useful, but they train
  different systems. Daniels VDOT clarifies: threshold = 1-hour race
  pace. Tempo can be slightly slower.
- **Long-run progression: km > pace.** Don't worry about your long-run
  pace. Build km. Pace develops automatically as VO₂ catches up.
- **The marathon-specific session is the 25k–32k long run with the
  last 8–12 km at goal MP.** This is the *single most predictive*
  session. If it goes well, the marathon goes well. Coach has built
  these into Wks 11, 14, and 16.

**Best episode:** "Lactate threshold for amateurs" or "Marathon-pace
work — when and how much."

### 9. **Ali on the Run Show** — Ali Feller

**What it is:** Long-form interviews with elite runners and writers.
Ali is a journalist + runner who's done 60+ marathons. Personalities
shine here — less science, more story. Feels like a long conversation
with smart, experienced runners.

**Concrete takeaways:**
- **Race-day routine starts the night before.** What time you eat
  dinner, what you wear to bed, where the kit lays out — all decided
  the day before, executed mechanically race morning. Removes
  decisions, conserves cognitive energy.
- **The "miserable middle" of the marathon is real and predictable.**
  Most marathoners hit km 24–32 hard. The athletes who PB *expect*
  it; the ones who don't *react* to it. Have a mental script ready.
- **Crowd support matters more than people admit.** race day has elite
  crowd density. *Use it.* Eye contact at km 35–40 with strangers
  cheering you on is real fuel. Don't run with headphones in the
  final 10 km.

**Best episode:** Interviews with Des Linden (4-time NYC, won Boston),
Gabriele Grunewald (RIP — best for mental game), Sara Hall.

### 10. **Rich Roll Podcast** — Rich Roll (selected episodes only)

**What it is:** Long-form interview podcast — 2-3 hours per episode.
Hit-and-miss; many episodes drift into wellness-industry territory.
*But* the right episodes (with athletes, scientists, mental-performance
coaches) are gold and offer perspective at a depth few other shows
achieve.

**Concrete takeaways:**
- **Endurance is built on consistency, not heroics.** Roll's recurring
  thesis (echoed by every elite he interviews): the people who win
  show up consistently for years. There are no shortcuts. The build
  is the build.
- **Identity precedes performance.** If you become a marathoner —
  someone who runs no matter what — performance follows. If you stay
  someone who *tries to run*, performance plateaus. Your Wk 1 body
  photo is identity work; treat it as such.
- **Recovery deserves equal billing with training.** Rich Roll routine:
  meditation, sleep, fuelling, mobility. The "training" is one of four
  pillars, not the whole house.

**Best episodes for you:**
- Eliud Kipchoge (the calmest 2-hour conversation ever recorded)
- Stacy Sims (female-athlete physiology)
- Steve Magness (the mental side)
- Cam Hanes (when you want pure consistency-as-religion)
- Avoid: anything with "biohacker" or "longevity" in the title — too
  much wellness drift.
